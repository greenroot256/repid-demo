Transfer Sats

In Chipnet it splits tBCH between your wallets: fund one with the faucet and transfer to the others. The transfer is P2PKH → P2PKH without OP_RETURN: it moves balance but creates no RepID fact.

    Console — the protocol step by step: each action on the left mints an on-chain fact that the Ledger on the right rebuilds instantly.

    Protocol flow

    01

    Mint identity

    RFC-001 — anchors an immutable NFT to the chosen wallet. The vault covenant locks a BCH collateral: it can never go down on-chain, it can be increased and it returns when the identity is deleted.

    02

    Record interaction

    RFC-002/003/004 — defines explicit roles, requires both parties' signatures; issues an Interaction Receipt and two Rating Rights.

    03

    Issue rating

    RFC-004 — spends a covenant-less Rating Right; the NFT is burned since it never reappears in any output.

    04

    Confirm interaction

    SPEC-003 / RF-06 — the platform corroborates that an interaction happened: it spends its own UTXO with an OP_RETURN referencing the Receipt. Standalone fact, without minting an Identity.

    05

    Declare trust

    SPEC-006 — unilateral declaration: the wallet on the left declares on-chain that it trusts the one on the right (without its consent, without Rating Rights).

    Example app — delivery for two users

    Mini delivery platform on the raw RepID API, with two simultaneous users (Client and Rider) sharing the same chain. Order, Receipt, ratings and endorsements are anchored fact by fact.

    How does this demo work?

    The client requests a service (their signature is captured in the request) and the rider receives a ride-available notification; upon accepting it, their signature joins the client's and the on-chain Receipt is anchored (the interaction). Then each party rates the other with its Rating Right (one per side, single use). Below, the identities surrounding the interaction: the platform that validates the ride happened and a trust identity (friend, ex-boss or coworker) giving its trust vote on either of them. Go back to the Console to see the protocol behind it in real time (Ledger and Indexer view).

    Indexer — reputation and Confidence Index

    The indexer recognizes on-chain facts without weighting. For every profile it shows two separate numbers: the Reputation (1–5 star average, always the same, depending on nobody's criterion) and the Confidence Index (CI, 0–1): the confidence this indexer assigns to that reputation according to on-chain signals it chooses and weights itself. The CI doesn't touch the stars: it's local indexer interpretation (SPEC-007).

    PROFILEReputation and CI of an identity

    Pick a wallet and a configuration, and click "View reputation and CI".

    CRITERIACI signals

    Choose which on-chain signals enter each profile's CI computation. In CONFIGURATION you define how much each chosen signal weighs; with no weighted signal the CI stays undefined (the reputation doesn't change).

    CONFIGURATIONSignal weights

    CI = weighted average of the active signals. Weight 0 = the signal doesn't count.

    Save this criterion with a name to reuse it in the Profile. The "Sample" preset (builtin, read-only) starts loaded to understand the tool.

      Minted identities

      Every identity is an immutable NFT anchored to a wallet, with its on-chain history (genesis, collateral top-ups and burns) rebuilt from the Ledger. The burned ones stop being active but their history stays auditable: returned collateral and burn tx.

      Mint identity

      RFC-001 — anchor an immutable NFT to a wallet. The vault covenant locks a BCH collateral: it never goes down on-chain, it can be increased and it returns to the owner when the identity is burned.

      Active identities

      Wallet with an active NFT and locked collateral. Increase the collateral (it can never drop on-chain) or burn the identity to recover it.

      An identity is "validated" by those who issued it a trust declaration (endorsement). The note appears on each card and the filter trims the list to the identities validated by those addresses.

        Burned identities

        NFT burned on-chain: the wallet can mint a new identity whenever it wants. Its collateral returned to the owner's wallet. The history is kept in data/repid-state.json and survives restarts; use "Reset demo" to clear it.
